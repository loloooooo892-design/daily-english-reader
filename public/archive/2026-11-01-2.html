Atmosfar is a giddy aerial exploration game after the heart of No Man's Sky, but I'm already trying to escape it

Taking flight in early access today

Oh hi there! Why yes, I am free to chat. Please excuse the wind noise, I'm currently tumbling to my doom in Atmosfar, after getting lost in a cloud bank and exhausting my fuel. This kind of thing happens to me regularly in Atmosfar - an open world survival game that plays out across clusters of floating islands, with players scooting about in adorable, propellor-powered airships.

Many of the islands are quite large, with proper landing pads and bases set up by NPC scavengers. Many of them teem with non-human life: rainforests full of nectar droplets and carnivorous plants, meadows of ponderous honking lizards, and mossy grottos where you'll find caches of batteries and possibly get eaten by mantids. It's important to make the most of these larger islands, gathering materials for crafting at your deployable campsite (which straps pleasingly to the back of your whirring fly-mobile). It's particularly important to mine hydrogen crystals you can refine into fuel.

None of that's for me, though! I am a true spirit of the sky. The only fuel I need is my imagination. The only objective marker I acknowledge is the omnidirectional Call to Adventure. Besides, when you do run out of fuel in Atmosfar, you're actually unlocking two exciting minigames. The first consists of leaping from the cockpit and vainly trying to jetpack to the nearest walkable surface. The second consists of racing to overtake your plummeting airship and re-enter the cockpit before you smash into any islands beneath. I'm playing that second minigame right now. Come to papa, my beautiful turboprop son! Ah, balls.

Atmosfar is out in Steam early access today, with plans to stay in early access for at least nine months. I think it has some juice, though I am admittedly already trying to play it against the grain - and at times, trying to do things its technology doesn't quite allow.

It casts you as an interplanetary pioneer who, due to some minor infraction, has been locked up in a factory: a dreary opening area with some corpo-satire elements, from which you are soon liberated by an electrical storm. Busting past the sentry drones, you scramble down a cliffside and discover your very first Wasp hovercar. From there, you have more or less free rein of the game's opening biome, though there's a critical path of tutorials and NPC encounters you'll want to follow for the sake of unlocks, like the jetpack.

The scattered islands do a fair job of luring you from your HUD objectives (the HUD itself is a bit murky, in any case – I struggled to make connections between waypoints and quests in my journal). Even if there's nothing to capital-i Interact with, it's a pleasure to alight on some drifting gobbet of soil and flowers, peer into the crevices, and maybe take a few photos of distant astral mountains.

The searing emptiness all around makes the design of each island feel more precise and careful, gift-wrapped for the player. I find that I'm most interested in the tinier islands, the ones that were presumably a secondary consideration for developers Apog Labs, and maybe handed off to a junior staffer. I gaze at a random grass tuft and try to guess how much ingenuity went into the placement of something many pilots will totally ignore.

I'm already chafing against the survival systems a bit, however. Based on a couple of hours of cloudsteading, Atmosfar's core ingredients aren't particularly novel or involving. There's no tree-chopping, thank god, but you will spend many moments pointing your extractor at a nugget of glowing resourcegook, splitting it into plastic sheets and steel girders and so forth.

Many of the opening missions involve toing and froing between deposits and your airship or camp. It's humdrum stuff. The game's non-player scavengers seem like quest-givers first, characters second. Your inventory size is limited, to begin with. Deeper in, there's the opportunity to construct a huge airbase, the Cloud Cruiser, which I'm really hoping doesn't need refuelling. Although it would be a great joke to accidentally drop one with several co-op buds inside.

I've spent most of my two hours in Atmosfar in headlong retreat from the tutorial quests, trying to reach places I sense I'm not supposed to, without paying my dues to the crafting system. This includes both the ruddier, volcanic areas higher up, which I do believe I can reach, if I bother to refine enough fuel. But I'm more captivated, right now, by what lies beneath.

During a nighttime mission to gather eggs for some outpost chef, I gave into curiosity and aimed my Wasp straight down, spiralling past lonely stalactites with no creature population, into a swirling turquoise abyss full of bare, indistinct rock. As I descended, the game's tech began to creak and fizzle, as though strained by increasing gravity. My ride's exhaust condensed and flattened into glitchy flakes.

I ignored the simulation's protests, holding my course like Ahab aiming a harpoon at Moby Dick's kisser, till a "leaving mission area" reset timer yanked me up short. I ascended to just above the cut-off point, and gawped at the horizon. A juddering white ring enclosed the world, surrounded by wrinkled LIDAR pulses that made me think of thoughts coursing through brain tissue.

Now, this was an adventure. I flapped around a bit, battling turbulence, and managed to land on one of the weirdly unadorned, formless boulders. Who made this? Some procedural generation system, or did somebody actually have to 'hand-craft' these places that are probably not for serious human inquiry or habitation? I shot a few flares over the side and tried to deploy my campsite, but there was too little space on the boulder. Then the sun rose, slowly staining the milky blue fastness pink, and revealing the crisper silhouettes of the properly designed and navigable islands high above.

That last screenshot is my favourite so far - the rosy, rugged beauty of Atmosfar and the bizarre quirks of its technology reaching a strangely persuasive accord. There's only one issue: I've run out of fuel again. I guess I could yeet myself into the void and reset to the nearest landing pad, but that doesn't feel like something a true Spirit of the Sky would do. I guess I'll have to enlist another RPS scribe as a co-op partner and have them come fetch me. Perhaps they can complete the tutorials first and rock up in one of those Cloud Cruiser things. In the meantime, you can get your fill of Atmosfaring via Steam.
